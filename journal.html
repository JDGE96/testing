Through smiles, laughs, tears, and pain, you have and will always have a part of my heart. Another part of this letter is for the possibility that we eventually fade apart completely. I know you don't want that. And I truly believe you love me enough that you've wanted to do everything you can to keep me in your life. That's part of why I'm scared and why I'm writing this letter. Because I don't know what's going to happen. I don't want us to fade apart. But I also don't know what the future holds. Fair warning: this letter will be a lot. I also have a gift that comes with it. A card that shows off my new talent of building scripts, and with the help of the internet, I used that to build a website. I'll explain more about that later. I hope this letter helps and doesn't drive us further apart. I ask that you keep it safe if you do decide to keep it. This and the card are probably something that could "get you in trouble," and I don't want to do that. I don't want to ruin your future. I promise I don't. The purpose of this whole thing is simply to finally say the words I've been too scared to say out loud. You can do whatever you want with this letter. I also have a digital version of it in case you ever want or need it, or if you decide you don't want to keep the physical copy. Whatever you choose to do with it is okay. I've always loved how dramatic and worried you could get because underneath all of it was just how deeply you cared. I love how passionate you get about whatever hobby currently occupies your mind. I love how excited you got about trips we were planning. The plane ride. The train trip. Disney World. Sometimes you could get excited about something as simple as Chick-fil-A on a random Thursday. And one of my favorite things of all is how you run and hug me when you miss me. Every time we went to Princeton to meet, you'd do it. Heck, you even did it at the bowling alley. I could honestly keep going forever, but I'll run out of pages, and I still have a lot more I want to write about. There are so many other things I cherish about you that aren't even on these pages. But I hope when you read this, you remember something important: You are an amazing woman. Not because you're perfect. Not because you've never made mistakes. Not because of what you can do for me. But because of who you are. And if you ever need to be reminded of any of the things I cherish about you, you can ask me. I will always be happy to tell you. PART 2 - ME I'm sorry. I'm sorry for every time I got angry over something small. For every time I made you feel unloved and didn't realize it, or didn't do enough to help. I'm sorry for getting frustrated over things that didn't deserve my frustration. I'm sorry for letting little things become bigger things. I'm sorry for the times my emotions became something you had to deal with when they should have been mine to manage. I wish I had been able to understand some of these things while you were standing right in front of me asking me to understand them. I'm sorry for all the things you may have kept inside because you were worried I would get angry or frustrated. That hurts me to think about. You should have been able to tell me when I was hurting you. You should have felt safe enough to say, "This hurt me," without wondering what my reaction would be. If there are things I did wrong that you never felt safe enough to tell me about, I want you to know that you can be honest with me. I may not always immediately understand, but I want to listen. I want to take responsibility. I want to consider what you are saying instead of becoming defensive. I'm sorry that this past year I didn't give you more of myself because work drained me so badly. If I had known how much it would affect my mind, my energy, and ultimately us, I would have made different decisions. I regret the way I allowed work to consume parts of me that should have belonged to the people and things I loved. But most of all, I'm sorry that some of the things I understand now are things I should have understood while we were still together. I can't go back and change those moments. I can't give you back the time I didn't give you. I can't undo the arguments, the frustration, the missed opportunities, or the moments where you felt alone when I should have been beside you. All I can do is finally acknowledge them. And I am truly sorry. I'm sorry for all of it, Lil' Fox. I'm so sorry for all of it, Haylea. This year has been really hard on me with work, responsibilities, and everything else life was throwing at me. I let all of that cloud my depression and anxiety, and I let it take control of my own emotions. That's not an excuse for how I acted. It's just what was happening inside me, and I wish I had understood it better while I was going through it. I never should have allowed what I was dealing with to affect you or our relationship the way it did. I wanted so badly to build the perfect life for us. I wanted the perfect house. I wanted the perfect way for us to get married. I wanted us to have a life where compromises came easily instead of everything feeling like a struggle. I wanted to figure out how to take care of the animals, watch over Raven, visit both of our families, make time for each other, and somehow keep everything balanced and happy. I wanted to be able to handle all of it. And I put a lot of that responsibility on myself. Raven was a big part of that. I always felt bad asking someone else to watch her because I felt like she was my responsibility. I wanted her to be able to socialize with more people too, because I wanted to eventually be able to bring her with us instead of feeling like I had to stay tied down to that responsibility. When she needed medication, I felt like even more of a burden. I didn't want to ask anyone for help because I felt like I was supposed to handle it myself. Now I'm realizing that there are people who are willing to help, and I'm slowly learning that accepting help doesn't make me a burden or mean that I'm failing. I never wanted you to feel like a burden because of any of that. That's part of why I was okay with you visiting your family without me. It wasn't because I didn't want to go. I did. I love going up there. I wanted to be able to stay longer than just a weekend someday. I was getting myself closer to being able to do that with the remote job, and I wanted to save enough to eventually get a vehicle that would make traveling easier and let me bring Raven with us. I pictured us being able to go to Canada whenever we wanted. I pictured us having that freedom. I was trying to build toward those things, but somewhere along the way I became overwhelmed by the job, the depression, the anxiety, the responsibilities, and everything I was trying to hold together at once. I didn't want to burden you with what was happening inside me because you had your own struggles, and I wanted to be the person who took care of you rather than becoming another thing you had to take care of. But in trying not to burden you, I stopped communicating with you. And that was part of the problem. I kept things inside until they became too much. I let frustration build. I became bitter and cold. I let things affect how I treated you, even though you didn't deserve any of that. I'm sorry. I wish I had told you what was happening instead of trying so hard to carry it by myself. I kept telling myself I just needed to push through a little longer because I believed things would eventually get better and that we'd finally have the life we'd been working toward. But I didn't realize how much I was drowning emotionally while I was trying to get there. And I let that pain bleed into us. I'm not telling you any of this for pity, and I'm not telling you because I think it means I deserve another chance. I just want you to have a clearer understanding of what was happening inside my head and the things I was too scared or overwhelmed to say while we were together. I'm starting to understand myself better now. Getting help, praying, getting out of the house more, and learning how to communicate what I'm actually feeling have all helped me start opening up in ways I struggled to before. I know that doesn't erase what happened, and I know some of these realizations came too late to change the past. But they are steps in the right direction. And I want to keep taking those steps whether our paths come back together or not. If you ever ask me what I'm feeling, or why I did something, I want to be honest with you. I don't want to hide behind silence anymore. We made that agreement with each other—to be honest going forward. Even if you have to "Tortoise" me into doing it. lol. For us, Tortoise means dropping the shield. It means admitting when we're lying about our emotions, when we're pretending we're okay when we're not, when we need each other, or when there's something we're afraid to say. It means stopping and being completely honest. I actually love that we made that agreement. Because I think that's something I needed to learn a long time ago: that being honest about what I'm feeling doesn't make me weak, and asking for help doesn't make me a burden. So if you ever have to Tortoise me, do it. I'll listen. I'll be honest. And I'll try to understand before I react. I'm sorry that I struggled to tell you all of this before. I really am, Lil' Fox. I just wanted to finally communicate some of the things that had been in my head for a long time that I never really knew how to put into words. I wanted us to explore the world. I wanted to build a small place for us to stay near your parents and another near my family, or at least have a way to comfortably visit both of them for longer periods of time. I wanted a vehicle that could carry Raven, and eventually our children if we had them, so we could just go somewhere whenever we wanted. I wanted us to be able to explore. I wanted to spend two weeks at your mom's if we wanted to. I wanted us to have a little place near my family too. I wanted the freedom to travel, see new things, visit the people we loved, and still have a home to come back to. I wanted to be able to take care of us while still making sure all of us got to have fun and experience life. I wanted you to have the freedom to work because you wanted to, rather than feeling like you always had to. I was okay with you contributing in whatever way worked for you. I even imagined a life where we could homeschool our kids and spend more time exploring nature instead of feeling like every part of our lives had to revolve around a schedule. I wanted a life where we had some freedom. And I wanted our wedding at your parents' place more than anything. I honestly didn't care if all of my friends were there. I wanted your family to celebrate it with us because it was your day too, and I wanted to make it as special as I possibly could. I thought about all of these things so much that sometimes I was living years ahead in my head instead of paying enough attention to the day that was actually in front of me. I was always thinking about the house. The wedding. The vehicle. The trips. The family. The future. And somewhere along the way, I forgot that the future is built out of ordinary days. I started falling apart because of that job. I became so focused on trying to get us to the future I imagined that I didn't realize how much I was sacrificing in the present. There were times I seriously considered quitting because of how much it was taking from me and from my time with you. One of the biggest things I've learned through all of this is that everything doesn't have to be perfect. I used to think I needed to get everything lined up just right before I could really enjoy our life. The perfect house. The perfect financial situation. The perfect wedding. The perfect plans. The perfect future. I'm starting to understand that life doesn't work that way. The greatest thing about the medication, the work I've been doing on myself, and everything I've been learning through this is realizing that I don't have to make everything perfect before I allow myself to enjoy today. I should have focused more on today. I should have appreciated the ordinary days more. I should have spent more time simply being with you instead of constantly thinking about how I was going to build the life I thought we needed. I understand that now. And whether our futures ever become connected again or whether our lives eventually go in completely different directions, that's something I want to carry with me. I don't want to spend my life so focused on tomorrow that I forget to live today.
